Symptom Checker

Start With What Your Furnace Is Doing

Six common presentations, the usual cause behind each one, and what a technician checks first.

The furnace will not start

What it usually means

A thermostat that never calls, a tripped breaker, a failed igniter, or a safety switch holding the system off.

What we do

We trace the startup order from the thermostat to the burners and stop at the step that fails.

Call right away if

The house is cooling steadily and the forecast says the night gets colder.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.

Warm vents, cold rooms

What it usually means

The furnace makes heat but the air is not arriving: blower trouble, duct leaks, or a choked filter.

What we do

We compare supply temperatures room by room, then test blower output and static pressure.

Call right away if

One whole floor never warms while the furnace runs nonstop.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.

It starts, stops, starts again

What it usually means

Short cycling, most often an overheating furnace from blocked airflow or a flame signal that fades.

What we do

We watch a full cycle with meters on, catching the exact moment the system shuts itself down.

Call right away if

Cycles shrink to a few minutes and the house never reaches the set temperature.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.

New noises from the utility room

What it usually means

A bang hints at delayed ignition, a squeal at a worn blower bearing, a rattle at loose panels or duct metal.

What we do

We time the noise inside the heating cycle, which narrows it to a short list of parts.

Call right away if

The sound is a boom, a grinding scrape, or anything that started suddenly.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.

A smell when the heat runs

What it usually means

First-run dust in October is normal. Ongoing electrical, oil, or gas odors are not.

What we do

We inspect the burners, wiring, and heat exchanger, and check for gas leaks at the valve train.

Call right away if

You smell gas or the carbon monoxide detector sounds. Leave the house and call from outside.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.

The thermostat lies

What it usually means

Dead batteries, a sensor thrown off by sun or drafts, or a wire that lost its connection.

What we do

We test the signal at the furnace terminals, which separates wall problems from furnace problems in minutes.

Call right away if

The display is blank, the house is cold, and fresh batteries change nothing.

Call (703) 962-7410 and describe what yours is doing.
